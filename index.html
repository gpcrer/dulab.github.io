Short Bio

Dr. Yang Du is a tenured Associate Professor at the School of Medicine, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and a Principal Investigator at the Kobilka Institute of Innovative Drug Discovery (KIIDD). He received his B.Sc. from Xiangya School of Medicine, Central South University, and his Ph.D. from the University of Science and Technology of China.

In 2012 he joined the laboratory of Prof. Brian Kobilka — 2012 Nobel Laureate in Chemistry — at Stanford University, where his postdoctoral and staff-scientist research on G protein-coupled receptors was supported by a full fellowship from the American Heart Association. He was offered a tenure-track Assistant Professorship at the University of Michigan Medical School, but in 2017 he chose to help found the Kobilka Institute at CUHK-Shenzhen, establishing his independent group there in 2019.

His laboratory has authored more than 80 peer-reviewed papers, including first- or corresponding-author articles in Cell, Science, Cell Research, Cell Discovery, Cell Reports, Science Advances, Nature Communications, JACS and PNAS. As a co-inventor with Prof. Brian Shoichet (UCSF), he holds a U.S. patent on a non-addictive analgesic discovered through structure-based design. He is a Fellow of the Royal Society of Medicine (UK), a Chang-Jiang Young Scholar, and recipient of the inaugural Young Researcher Award at CUHK-Shenzhen.

03Research

Decoding the molecular logic
of GPCR signaling.

G protein-coupled receptors are the largest superfamily of human membrane proteins and the molecular targets of more than one third of all approved drugs. The Du laboratory integrates single-particle cryo-EM, X-ray crystallography, reconstitution biochemistry, and quantitative cellular pharmacology with deep-learning structure prediction (AlphaFold / RoseTTAFold-class), molecular-dynamics simulation and ultra-large-scale virtual screening of billion-compound chemical libraries.

— I. Mechanism

Atomic-resolution structures of receptor — transducer complexes

High-resolution cryo-EM and X-ray structures of therapeutically important GPCRs bound to endogenous ligands, drug candidates, and downstream transducers (Gs, Gi/o, Gq, β-arrestin). Recent contributions include the V2-vasopressin–Gs, TGR5–Gs, GPR17–Gi, GPR21, GPR88, GPR3 and the kisspeptin receptor complexes.

Cryo-EMX-rayClass A GPCRHeterotrimeric G proteins
— II. Pharmacology

Biased, allosteric & orphan receptor signaling

By stabilizing distinct functional states of pharmacologically challenging receptors, the lab maps the conformational logic that links ligand chemistry to G-protein- versus arrestin-biased outputs, and identifies allosteric pockets that can be leveraged to design pathway-selective drugs with cleaner therapeutic windows.

Biased agonismAllosteryOrphan GPCRsConformational dynamics
— III. Discovery

AI- & structure-based discovery of safer therapeutics

In long-standing collaboration with Prof. Brian Shoichet (UCSF), the group has contributed to the structure-guided design of non-addictive analgesics — including μ-opioid receptor agonists (e.g. PZM21) and α2A-AR agonists that decouple analgesia from sedation, respiratory depression and reward — powered by ultra-large-library docking and deep-learning structure prediction.
